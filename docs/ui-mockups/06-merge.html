THE HISTORY OF LOGIC

The turn of the century brought with it a renewed confidence in formal method. Where the older textbooks had treated logic as a branch of psychology, the new manuals treated it as a discipline in its own right, with its own standards of rigour and its own characteristic problems.

In the early yeers of the century, logicians turned their attention to the relation between language and the world, and the Vienna Circle held that a statement which cannot be verified by experience is without sense.

It is worth remarking that the older tradition did not disappear at once. In many universities the classical syllabus was taught side by side with the new logic, and students were left to reconcile the two as best they could.
