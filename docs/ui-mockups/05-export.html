Chapter 4 — The Early Years
In the early years of the century, logicians turned their attention to the relation between language and the world. The question was no longer whether our thoughts mirror reality, but whether the very structure of our sentences could be said to picture the facts they describe. This shift, subtle as it may seem, changed the whole direction of philosophical inquiry.

It is worth remarking, finally, that the older tradition did not disappear at once. In many universities the classical syllabus was taught side by side with the new logic.
